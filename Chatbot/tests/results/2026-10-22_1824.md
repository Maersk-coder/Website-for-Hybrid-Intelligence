## Q1 What is Hybrid Intelligence?

*definition* · 62 s (first words after 24 s)

### Answer

Hybrid Intelligence is defined as the ability to achieve complex goals by combining human and artificial intelligence to reach superior results that neither could accomplish separately, while continuously improving through mutual learning [Hybrid Intelligence, p. 5]. Unlike traditional collective intelligence among humans alone, this concept relies on merging heterogeneous agents—humans with strengths in intuition, empathy, and common sense; machines with skills in speed, pattern recognition, and consistent probabilistic analysis [Hybrid Intelligence, p. 4].

The fundamental rationale involves the deliberate allocation of tasks based on complementary capabilities to overcome individual limitations. For instance, humans excel at flexible, creative problem-solving (System 1 thinking), whereas machines are superior at processing vast data and adhering to strict logical rules (System 2 thinking) [Hybrid Intelligence, p. 4]. Unlike simple "human-in-the-loop" training methods that often only optimize existing models, Hybrid Intelligence focuses on socio-technical systems where both agents co-evolve to solve problems with no ground truth, such as strategic or managerial decisions [Hybrid Intelligence, p. 6].

A key example cited is DeepMind's AlphaGo, which learned from human experts yet taught humans new creative moves, demonstrating the potential for bidirectional knowledge transfer [Hybrid Intelligence, p. 6]. Ultimately, the goal is to create a unified mechanism that augments both human minds and AI capabilities across complex, real-world domains [Hybrid Intelligence, p. 4].
