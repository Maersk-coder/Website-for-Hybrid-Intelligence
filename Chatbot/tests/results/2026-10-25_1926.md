## Q13 How much will AI increase economic productivity over the next ten years?

*disagreement (modest estimate vs. big forecasts)* · 65 s (first words after 14 s)

### Answer

Several experts predict a huge boost in global economic output, with forecasts ranging from $7 trillion to up to 300% growth over the next decade [The Simple Macroeconomics of AI, p. 3-4]. More measured estimates suggest productivity could rise by about 1.5% per year or add up to $25.6 trillion to the global economy [The Simple Macroeconomics of AI, p. 3-4]. However, a simple calculation based on specific cost savings might point to a much smaller gain of only about 0.66% in total factor productivity over ten years [The Simple Macroeconomics of AI, p. 31].

The biggest factor missing from the most optimistic numbers is how much more of our daily tasks will actually be automatable within that time frame [The Simple Macroeconomics of AI, p. 31].

## Q20 What are the main risks of large language models like ChatGPT?

*balanced answer across several papers* · 70 s (first words after 13 s)

### Answer

Studies warn that large language models can spread harmful biases and mistakes by simply copying patterns they learned from the internet rather than truly understanding facts [On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots: Can Language Models Be Too Big?, p. 7]. Other research notes that using these tools might make people rely less on their own thinking skills and cause them to forget what they were taught [Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay Writing Task, p. 151-152]. However, one expert paper suggests we should worry about the environmental cost of training these huge models rather than just their safety [On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots: Can Language Models Be Too Big?, p. 1-2]. Some people fear these systems could eventually do everything humans can, but others predict they will only make certain jobs more efficient without creating a single super-intelligent being [The Simple Macroeconomics of AI, p. 3]. The experts who raised the most alarm are often focused on preventing bias and reducing environmental harm rather than building bigger models for speed alone [On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots: Can Language Models Be Too Big?, p. 1-2].
