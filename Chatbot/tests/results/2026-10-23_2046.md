## Q5 When does combining humans and AI work best?

*conditions* · 35 s (first words after 5 s)

### Answer

Combining humans and AI works best when you need to create new content rather than make simple decisions [When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis, p. 3]. Research shows that teams often perform worse together on decision tasks, but they can outperform a human working alone on creative projects like writing or art [When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis, p. 1]. True "synergy," where the combined group beats both individuals acting alone, happens most clearly in creation tasks because AI handles routine details while humans provide the final creative inspiration [When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis, p. 6]. In contrast, using AI for decisions is generally less effective unless the human expert is doing better than the machine on its own [When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis, p. 1]. An example is AlphaGo, which learned from human moves to become superhuman but also taught new strategies back to its human players [Hybrid Intelligence, p. 6].

## Q6 Why is that?

### Answer

It can also work well when humans are better than the AI alone, because then they learn to trust their own judgment over the machine's advice [When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis, p. 5]. For instance, one study showed that while the AI alone was accurate at spotting fake hotel reviews, adding a human who couldn't trust the algorithm actually lowered the group's performance [When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis, p. 5].
