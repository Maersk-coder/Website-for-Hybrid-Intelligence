## Q3 Do humans and AI perform better together than alone?

*disagreement / benefits and risks* · 73 s (first words after 31 s)

### Answer

A major study found that, on average, human-AI groups performed worse than the best single performer (either the human or the AI) alone [When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis, p. 1]. While some tasks like coding saw faster results with tools like GitHub Copilot, other situations resulted in overall lower accuracy when people combined their judgment with AI suggestions [Generative AI at Work, p. 6]. This happens because if a human is less accurate than the AI, they often fail to trust the machine correctly, causing their own mistakes to mix with the AI's good predictions and lowering the final result [When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis, p. 5]. Conversely, when humans are naturally more skilled in a specific area, adding AI can push performance even higher, such as reaching 90% accuracy in bird image classification where it jumped from 81% for humans alone [When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis, p. 5].

## Q15 Is AI good or bad for workers?

*balanced answer across several papers* · 29 s (first words after 13 s)

### Answer

Studies show that AI can boost worker productivity and help them learn new skills, especially for those with less experience [Generative AI at Work, p. 35-36]. However, other research finds that AI can sometimes make workers perform worse because they rely too heavily on its suggestions or get confused by mixed results [Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality, p. 18]. The overall effect seems to depend on the type of job and how much workers use the tool rather than AI being simply good or bad everywhere.

## Q19 Does relying on AI tools affect people's critical thinking?

*risks* · 60 s (first words after 12 s)

### Answer

Multiple studies show that using AI tools can lower people's ability to think critically because it encourages them to offload mental work [AI Tools in Society: Impacts on Cognitive Offloading and the Future of Critical Thinking, p. 15]. A large survey found that frequent users scored lower on critical thinking tests than those who rarely used AI [AI Tools in Society: Impacts on Cognitive Offloading and the Future of Critical Thinking, p. 1]. Even brain scans suggest that relying too much on AI weakens the neural circuits needed for deep problem-solving [Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay Writing Task, p. 122]. However, some research indicates that highly educated people might be better able to use AI without losing these skills [AI Tools in Society: Impacts on Cognitive Offloading and the Future of Critical Thinking, p. 16], and other work suggests AI can sometimes boost productivity if used correctly [Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality, p. 4]. The overall picture shows a trade-off where AI helps with speed but often comes at the cost of independent thought [Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay Writing Task, p. 10].
