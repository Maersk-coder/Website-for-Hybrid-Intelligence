### Answer

Combining humans and AI generally leads to performance losses compared to using the best of either alone, but it works best under specific conditions where their complementary strengths are leveraged. Research indicates that synergy (where the team outperforms both individuals) is significantly more likely in **creation tasks** rather than decision tasks [When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis, p. 4-5]. Specifically, when humans outperform AI alone, combining them yields performance gains because humans tend to make better judgments about when to trust their own intuition versus the algorithm [When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis, p. 4-5]. Conversely, when AI alone is superior, adding humans often degrades overall performance, particularly if humans struggle to identify when to rely on the expert system [When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis, p. 5].

Furthermore, significant improvements occur only when processes involve a deliberate division of labor, assigning specific subtasks to whichever partner (human or machine) is better suited for them [When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis, p. 4-5]. Interestingly, providing explanations or confidence levels from the AI did not significantly impact collaboration outcomes, suggesting that simple information sharing is insufficient for true synergy [When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis, p. 2-3]. For optimal results, future systems should focus on generative tasks, innovative processes, and robust metrics that account for the relative strengths of each agent rather than relying solely on trust mechanisms [When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis, p. 6; Hybrid Intelligence, p. 5-6].
