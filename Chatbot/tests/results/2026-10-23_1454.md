## Q12 What is the "jagged technological frontier"?

*definition* · 44 s (first words after 26 s)

### Answer

The "jagged technological frontier" describes how AI can be surprisingly good at some things while failing completely at others, like making simple counting errors or getting tricked by tricky questions [Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality, p. 18-19]. This means experts might do worse when they rely too much on AI because the technology is unreliable in specific ways [Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality, p. 18-19]. Even tech leaders and engineers now agree that humans must stay carefully involved to catch these errors [Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality, p. 18-19].

## Q13 How much will AI increase economic productivity over the next ten years?

*disagreement (modest estimate vs. big forecasts)* · 35 s (first words after 11 s)

### Answer

Research papers predict AI could raise global GDP growth by 0.93% to 1.56% over the next ten years if investment increases moderately to significantly [The Simple Macroeconomics of AI, p. 45-47]. Some experts forecast even larger gains, like a rise between 1.5% and 3.4 percentage points in annual GDP growth for advanced economies [The Simple Macroeconomics of AI, p. 3-5]. However, the authors of one study argue their estimates might be modest because early results mainly came from easy tasks rather than harder ones [The Simple Macroeconomics of AI, p. 2-3]. Other sources claim these large effects are plausible due to revolutionary changes in how work gets done [The Simple Macroeconomics of AI, p. 3-5].

## Q15 Is AI good or bad for workers?

*balanced answer across several papers* · 40 s (first words after 11 s)

### Answer

Research on AI's impact on workers shows mixed results depending on the context and type of worker. Studies find that generative AI can significantly boost productivity for less-skilled workers in tasks like customer support by helping them resolve issues faster [Generative AI at Work, p. 3-5]. In contrast, other research suggests that while some low-skill workers benefit, overall wage growth may be limited and inequality between capital owners and labor is likely to increase because technology makes labor cheaper relative to capital [The Simple Macroeconomics of AI, p. 29]. Additionally, there is a risk that experienced workers might rely too heavily on AI, causing their own performance to drop slightly [Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality, p. 18-19].

Because different studies highlight both benefits and risks, the overall effect depends heavily on how specific tasks are structured and whether new jobs are created to offset job losses [The Simple Macroeconomics of AI, p. 29].
